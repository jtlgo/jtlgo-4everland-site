The Six Buckets of Landed Cost When Buying From China

Landed cost is often described as a list of six buckets: goods, China delivery, purchasing service, warehouse work, international transport and destination charges. Knowing the list is easy. Filling it in honestly is harder, because each bucket has its own way of being underestimated. This article goes bucket by bucket and names the most common buyer mistake in each, then shows a simple worksheet layout you can reuse for every order.

Bucket one and two: goods and China delivery

Goods. The mistake here is using the listing price instead of the confirmed price. Marketplace listings show a price for one variant, often the cheapest. The colour, size or bundle you actually need may cost more. Seller confirmation also decides final availability, so a price is only firm once the seller has confirmed stock.

China delivery. Domestic shipping from the seller to the warehouse is a separate line. The mistake is assuming it is free because the listing says so for local buyers. Some sellers include it, others charge per parcel, and multi-seller orders multiply it. Put a line for each seller, even if some are zero.

Bucket three: the purchasing service

On JTLGO's published pricing, the purchasing service fee is 10% of product value with a minimum of USD 10 per order, and no minimum product value. Above USD 10,000 a specialist can discuss a volume discount.

The mistake is applying 10% to the total bill rather than to product value. The fee is based on product value only; shipping, duty and handling are quoted separately. The second mistake is forgetting the minimum. A USD 40 order pays the USD 10 minimum, which is 25% of product value, not 10%.

Bucket four: warehouse work

Warehouse work is where many buyers have no number at all. The purchasing fee includes consolidation planning, packing confirmation, basic QC photos and up to seven days of storage, but physical consolidation, unpacking, packing materials, repacking and longer storage follow the warehouse charge sheet.

JTLGO publishes current core rates, for example consolidation and unpacking at USD 0.50 per kg of chargeable weight and standard packing materials at USD 0.30 per kg of volumetric weight with a USD 1 minimum and USD 5 maximum per package. There is also a minimum service charge: if handling and packing together come to less than USD 5, the bill is topped up to USD 5.

The mistake is leaving this bucket blank because "the agent handles it". Estimate it from the charge sheet using your expected weight.

Bucket five: international transport

International shipping is billed after the parcel is weighed at the warehouse, with differences refunded or charged. The mistake is using product weight. Chargeable weight depends on packed cartons, and volume can dominate for light goods. Enter an estimate, label it as such, and expect an adjustment.

Bucket six: destination charges

Under a duty-unpaid term, duty, tax and any brokerage arrive at the destination. Under a duty-paid term they sit inside the freight line. The mistake is counting them twice or not at all. Decide which term you are using, then put destination charges in exactly one place.

A reusable worksheet layout

Bucket Source of the number Status
Goods Seller-confirmed price per variant Confirmed / estimate
China delivery Seller quote per parcel Confirmed / estimate
Purchasing service 10% of goods, minimum USD 10 Calculated
Warehouse work Charge sheet × expected weight Estimate
International transport Quote, final after weighing Estimate until weighed
Destination Duty term decides where it sits Estimate

Add a final column for the actual amount once the order is complete. Comparing estimate and actual on each order is the fastest way to improve the next estimate.

Three warning signs your landed cost is too optimistic

  • More than one bucket is blank.
  • The international line uses product weight instead of packed weight.
  • The destination line is missing on a duty-unpaid quote.

Handling large or unusual cargo in the worksheet

If any carton is 50 kg or more, or 0.5 CBM or more, the warehouse handles it under large-cargo rates, and storage after the free period is charged per half cubic metre per day rather than per kilogram. Wooden crates, frames and pallets also have their own prices. Do not fold these into a general "warehouse" guess. Give them their own row, because they can outweigh every other line in bucket four. The same applies to detailed inspection photos or quantity counts that go beyond the basic QC included in the purchasing fee. Each extra service you request should appear as a named row with its own estimate, so the final bill holds no unexplained totals.

FAQ

Is the purchasing fee charged on shipping costs?

No. It is based on product value only.

What does the USD 10 minimum mean for small orders?

On orders under USD 100 of product value, the fee is USD 10, which is more than 10% of the goods.

Which bucket is most often underestimated?

Warehouse work, because buyers assume it is included. Only coordination and up to seven days of storage are included in the purchasing fee.

Why should I track actual against estimate?

Because it shows which bucket you consistently misjudge, so the next order is planned more accurately.
