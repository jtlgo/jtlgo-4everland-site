The Six Buckets of Landed Cost When Buying From China

A single "shipping price" rarely explains why two China orders with similar products end at different totals. JTLGO groups landed cost into six buckets on its China sourcing landed cost guidance: goods, China delivery, purchasing service, warehouse work, international transport and destination charges. Keeping those buckets separate is the fastest way to compare quotes honestly.

Carton on a scale next to a fee breakdown page. Image: jtlgo.com.

Bucket 1 and 2: goods and China delivery

Goods are the supplier invoice for the products themselves. China delivery is the domestic move from seller to the warehouse or consolidation point. Marketplace listings often hide domestic freight inside "free shipping" wording that only applies inside China. When an agent buys for you, ask for the domestic line to stay visible so international freight is not blamed for a local courier charge.

Bucket 3: purchasing service

JTLGO's published purchasing service fee is 10% of product value, minimum USD 10 per order, with no minimum product value. Above USD 10,000 the site says to contact a specialist for volume discount. Included in that purchasing scope, per the pricing page, are supplier communication, purchasing, basic QC photos and videos, consolidation planning, packing requirement confirmation and up to 7 days warehouse storage. Knowing what the percentage buys prevents double-counting warehouse photos later.

Warehouse conveyor, packing bench and pallets in one view. Image: jtlgo.com.

Bucket 4: warehouse work beyond the included scope

Physical consolidation, unpacking, repacking, packing materials, wooden crates, extra QC and storage beyond 7 days are listed as separate. FBA prep steps such as SKU separation and label checks also sit here when requested. Photo checks do not replace lab or full factory inspection; if you need that deeper work, budget it explicitly.

Bucket 5 and 6: international transport and destination

International transport is air, sea or express after export packing. JTLGO bills it after warehouse weighing, refunding or charging differences. Destination charges are duty, tax and related import costs. Under DDP those destination charges are prepaid inside the freight price; under DAP-style terms they may appear later. The Incoterm therefore decides which bucket absorbs destination cash, not whether destination rules exist.

Using the buckets in a comparison

When two agents quote different totals, map each line into the six buckets before declaring a winner. A cheaper international line that excludes duty is not cheaper than a DDP line. A lower purchasing percentage that excludes basic photos may shift cost into warehouse work. The pricing page and the landed-cost blog are the checklists; your spreadsheet is the tool.

Further practical notes for this topic

Building a reusable landed-cost sheet

Create six columns matching the buckets and reuse them for every China order. Enter supplier invoices under goods, domestic couriers under China delivery, the purchasing percentage under purchasing service, materials and extra QC under warehouse work, the weighed freight under international transport, and duty treatment under destination. Totals become comparable across months.

When a quote arrives as a single lump sum, ask the sender to split it. Lump sums hide whether duty is included and whether packing materials were forgotten. JTLGO's own pricing page already separates purchasing inclusions from freight and special packaging; mirror that discipline in your sheet.

Discount conversations above USD 10,000

The pricing page invites specialist contact for volume discount above USD 10,000 product value while stating the standard fee remains 10% until a discount is approved. Enter the standard fee in your sheet until the approval exists in writing. Optimistic discount assumptions break purchasing-budget forecasts.

FAQ

What are the six landed-cost buckets JTLGO describes?

Goods, China delivery, purchasing service, warehouse work, international transport and destination charges.

Is the 10% fee the whole landed cost?

No. It is the purchasing service bucket only, with a published minimum of USD 10.

Does DDP remove the destination bucket?

It prepays duty and tax inside the freight price; it does not remove destination rules or the need to declare goods correctly.
